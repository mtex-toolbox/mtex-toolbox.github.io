--- title: 'Cracks and Pores' sidebar: documentation_sidebar permalink: GrainReconstructionCracks.html folder: documentation toc: false --- Cracks and Pores edit page

A map of a natural rock or a sintered material rarely consists of grains alone. Wherever no pattern could be indexed, the map is notIndexed. On a specimen surface that happens at cracks, at pores and where material was plucked out during sample preparation. This page shows what calcGrains does with these regions, how to close the ones that are not of interest, and how to bridge a crack when the grains on its two sides belong together. It assumes the basic reconstruction from Grain Reconstruction.

The data

The map is a thin section of a peridotite, a mantle rock made mainly of olivine (forsterite), orthopyroxene (enstatite) and clinopyroxene (diopside). The step size is 60 micron, so the map is about 33 mm wide. The data are courtesy of David Mainprice, Géosciences Montpellier.

{% highlight matlab %} mtexdata peridotite silent plot(ebsd,'micronbar','off') {% endhighlight %}
{% include inline_image.html file="GrainReconstructionCracks_01.png" %}

The white regions are notIndexed, and they are of several kinds. A crack crosses the left half of the map from the bottom edge to the top edge, wide in its lower part and narrow in its upper part. Round holes inside some of the large forsterite grains are pores. Irregular gaps between several grains are pockets. White patches of a few pixels, scattered over the whole map, are specks. The white regions cut by the edge of the map, at the bottom and on the right, are voids.

The default reconstruction

We reconstruct the grains with a threshold angle of 15 degree and leave the treatment of the notIndexed regions at its defaults. The second output is the map with the grainId of every pixel; a pixel outside all grains has the grainId 0.

{% highlight matlab %} [grains,ebsdGrains] = calcGrains(ebsd,'angle',15*degree,'minPixel',5); grains = smoothBoundary(grains); fprintf('%d grains, %d notIndexed, %.1f%% of the map outside all grains\n', ... length(grains), length(grains('notIndexed')), ... 100*nnz(ebsdGrains.grainId==0)/length(ebsd)) plot(ebsd,'micronbar','off') legend off hold on plot(grains.boundary,'linewidth',1.5) plot(grains('notIndexed'),'FaceColor','black','FaceAlpha',0.35) hold off {% endhighlight %} {% highlight plaintext %} 540 grains, 34 notIndexed, 10.6% of the map outside all grains {% endhighlight %}
{% include inline_image.html file="GrainReconstructionCracks_02.png" %}

Each kind has ended up in a different place. The specks and the pores are inside the grains: they show white, without a boundary around them, and the grain continues around them. The pockets are the darkened regions. Each is a notIndexed grain of its own, with a boundary to every grain around it. The crack and the voids are outside all grains: white regions with a boundary along each wall. The crack is whole from its mouth at the bottom edge to the top edge, also where it is only a few pixels wide.

This is usually what one wants. A pore is a hole in a grain and its area belongs to that grain. A crack is a fracture, so the grains stop at its walls. A pocket is material that was not indexed, and it is a question of the map whether it should be a grain of its own.

The kinds of notIndexed regions

calcGrains sorts the notIndexed regions into these kinds and treats every kind by a rule of its own. The command classifyNotIndexed returns the kind of every pixel, so we can look at what calcGrains saw. We plot the kinds in colour over the indexed pixels in light grey.

{% highlight matlab %} kinds = classifyNotIndexed(ebsd,'angle',15*degree); names = ["pore" "pocket" "crack" "void" "speck"]; color = [0.2 0.4 1; 1 0.6 0; 1 0 0; 0 0 0; 0.55 0.55 0.55]; plot(ebsd('indexed'),'FaceColor',[0.9 0.9 0.9],'micronbar','off') legend off hold on for k = 1:5 plot(ebsd(kinds == names(k)),'FaceColor',color(k,:),'DisplayName',names(k)) end hold off {% endhighlight %}
{% include inline_image.html file="GrainReconstructionCracks_03.png" %}

The whole crack is one red band, from its mouth at the bottom edge to the top edge. The round pores inside the large grains are blue, the pockets between the grains are orange and the regions cut by the map edge are black. The grey specks are everywhere; they make up more than half of all notIndexed pixels.

Each kind is closed with a radius of its own, in pixels. A region narrower than twice its radius is filled, a wider one keeps its core.

  • Specks are what the radius alpha fills, 3.1 pixels by default, as in Grain Reconstruction.
  • Pores are closed with 'alphaPore', which is inf by default: a pore is filled whatever its size.
  • Pockets are closed with 'alphaPocket', which is alpha by default: a pocket that alpha leaves open stays open.
  • Cracks are closed with 'alphaCrack', which is 0 by default: a crack is never closed, not even where it is narrower than 2*alpha.
  • Voids are never closed.

Closing the pockets

Often the pockets are not of interest, and we want a map of grains without notIndexed grains in between, while the crack stays open. We set 'alphaPocket' to 10, which fills every pocket narrower than 20 pixels.

{% highlight matlab %} [grainsP,ebsdP] = calcGrains(ebsd,'angle',15*degree,'minPixel',5, ... 'alphaPocket',10); grainsP = smoothBoundary(grainsP); fprintf('%d grains, %d notIndexed, %.1f%% of the map outside all grains\n', ... length(grainsP), length(grainsP('notIndexed')), ... 100*nnz(ebsdP.grainId==0)/length(ebsd)) plot(ebsd,'micronbar','off') legend off hold on plot(grainsP.boundary,'linewidth',1.5) plot(grainsP('notIndexed'),'FaceColor','black','FaceAlpha',0.35) hold off {% endhighlight %} {% highlight plaintext %} 499 grains, 2 notIndexed, 10.0% of the map outside all grains {% endhighlight %}
{% include inline_image.html file="GrainReconstructionCracks_04.png" %}

The pockets have been shared out between the grains around them. Where several grains meet in a former pocket, their boundaries run through it as straight lines, the shortest boundaries the filled region allows. Only the two widest pockets keep a core as a notIndexed grain, and 'alphaPocket',inf fills those as well. The crack is as open as before.

Bridging the crack

A crack came after the grains, so the pieces on its two sides may be parts of one former grain. To count them as one grain, we bridge the crack. We set 'alphaCrack' to 15, which bridges the crack wherever it is narrower than 30 pixels. Where the two sides have nearly the same orientation they become one grain. Where they differ by more than the threshold angle, a new boundary runs through the crack.

{% highlight matlab %} [grainsC,ebsdC] = calcGrains(ebsd,'angle',15*degree,'minPixel',5, ... 'alphaPocket',10,'alphaCrack',15); grainsC = smoothBoundary(grainsC); fprintf('%d grains, %d notIndexed, %.1f%% of the map outside all grains\n', ... length(grainsC), length(grainsC('notIndexed')), ... 100*nnz(ebsdC.grainId==0)/length(ebsd)) {% endhighlight %} {% highlight plaintext %} 484 grains, 2 notIndexed, 7.5% of the map outside all grains {% endhighlight %}

We look at the crack in an IPF map, where the colour of a pixel is the crystal direction pointing out of the map, as in IPF Maps. Two pieces of one grain have the same colour. The boundaries of the reconstruction with the pockets closed are red, the new boundaries black.

{% highlight matlab %} plot(ebsd,'ipfz','micronbar','off') legend off hold on plot(grainsP.boundary,'linewidth',4,'LineColor','white') plot(grainsP.boundary,'linewidth',2,'LineColor','red') plot(grainsC.boundary,'linewidth',2) hold off {% endhighlight %}
{% include inline_image.html file="GrainReconstructionCracks_05.png" %}

The red walls of the crack have no black boundary on them any more: the grains reach across. Where a grain on the left has the same colour as its neighbour on the right, no boundary separates them, and the two pieces are one grain. Where the colours differ, a black boundary crosses the crack as a straight line between the points where it meets the two walls. Only the mouth of the crack at the bottom edge, wider than 30 pixels, remains outside all grains.

Correcting a kind

The kinds are read from the shape and the surroundings of a region, and they can be wrong for a map. kinds is a categorical array with one entry per pixel, so it can be edited and handed back to calcGrains with the option 'kinds'. Every region then takes the kind that its pixels say. To treat the crack as a void, for example, so that 'alphaCrack' leaves it alone:

{% highlight matlab %} kinds(kinds == "crack") = "void"; grains = calcGrains(ebsd,'angle',15*degree,'minPixel',5,'kinds',kinds); {% endhighlight %}

How the kinds are told apart

The closing with alpha fills every notIndexed region narrower than 2*alpha pixels; what it fills are the specks. What it leaves open are the thick parts of the white regions, and each of them gets its kind from its shape and its surroundings, in this order. A region that is long compared with its area and walled by grains is a crack: the square of its longest diameter is at least five times its area, and at least half of its boundary runs along grains. A region touching the edge of the map is a void. A region whose rim is held by one grain is a pore: at least 85 percent of the indexed pixels around it are connected by the same criterion that calcGrains uses to join pixels into grains. Every other region is a pocket. Finally, whatever lies between two pieces of a crack, joined to both by notIndexed pixels, counts as part of the crack, so that a crack stays whole where it widens and where it narrows.
