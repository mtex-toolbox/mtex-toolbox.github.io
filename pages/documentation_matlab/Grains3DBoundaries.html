--- title: 'The Three-Dimensional Boundary Network' sidebar: documentation_sidebar permalink: Grains3DBoundaries.html folder: documentation toc: false --- The Three-Dimensional Boundary Network edit page

The boundary of a set of three-dimensional grains is one surface mesh for the whole volume. Every face separates two grains and, when the grains were reconstructed from voxels, knows the two voxels it separates. Along the edges where three grains meet run the triple lines, and where four grains meet sits a quadruple point. This page reads the network of a reconstructed volume face by face, edge by edge and vertex by vertex.

{% highlight matlab %} plottingConvention.default('y↑→x'); how2plot = plottingConvention.default3D; fname = fullfile(mtexDataPath,'EBSD','SmallIN100_MeshStats.dream3d'); ebsd = EBSD3.load(fname); [grains,ebsd] = calcGrains(ebsd,'angle',5*degree); gB = grains.boundary {% endhighlight %} {% highlight plaintext %} gB = grain3Boundary (y↑→x) faces mineral 1 mineral 2 area 120000 unknown notIndexed 16% 637562 unknown unknown 84% {% endhighlight %}

One face, two grains, two voxels

A face is a triangle, two per voxel face. grainId holds the two grains it separates, with the face normal pointing from the first into the second. ebsdId holds the two voxels on either side, and misorientation the misorientation between their orientations, computed from the voxels rather than from the grain means. Faces on the outer hull of the volume have a zero in the second column of both.

{% highlight matlab %} [gB.grainId(1:5,:), gB.ebsdId(1:5,:)] {% endhighlight %} {% highlight plaintext %} ans = 150 56 21 22 150 56 21 22 56 137 26 27 56 137 26 27 137 51 35 36 {% endhighlight %}

The share of faces on the hull, and the share between indexed grains.

Select faces

A boundary is selected like a list: by the grains at its sides, by phase, or by any property of the faces. The faces of one grain, the faces between two grains, and the faces above a misorientation angle are three common selections.

{% highlight matlab %} [~,id] = max(grains.volume); gBid = gB(any(gB.grainId == id,2)); neighbours = setdiff(unique(gBid.grainId(:)),[0 id]).'; gBpair = gB(any(gB.grainId == id,2) & any(gB.grainId == neighbours(1),2)); gBhigh = gB(gB.misorientation.angle > 30*degree & all(gB.grainId > 0,2)); [length(gBid), length(gBpair), length(gBhigh)] {% endhighlight %} {% highlight plaintext %} ans = 16524 300 547682 {% endhighlight %}

The largest grain, with its faces coloured by the misorientation angle across them. The angle is uniform on each face between two grains and changes where a neighbour changes, so the colour draws the faces of the polyhedron.

{% highlight matlab %} plot(gBid,gBid.misorientation.angle./degree,'LineStyle','none','micronbar','off') setCamera(how2plot) mtexColorbar('title','misorientation angle in degree') {% endhighlight %}

Edges, triple lines and quadruple points

edges lists every edge once and tells for each face which three edges bound it. An edge on two faces lies inside a boundary face, an edge on three or more faces on a triple line. nodeType counts the grains at every vertex: 2 inside a face, 3 on a triple line, 4 at a quadruple point, and 10 more for a vertex on the outer hull.

{% highlight matlab %} [E,F2E] = edges(gB); isTripleLine = accumarray(F2E(:),1) >= 3; t = nodeType(gB); [size(E,1), nnz(isTripleLine), nnz(mod(t,10) == 3), nnz(mod(t,10) >= 4)] {% endhighlight %} {% highlight plaintext %} ans = 1103992 62789 42406 4004 {% endhighlight %}

The triple lines of the largest grain, drawn over its faces. Every line is where a neighbour ends and the next one begins, and the lines meet at the quadruple points.

{% highlight matlab %} V = gB.allV.xyz; onGrain = false(size(V,1),1); onGrain(gBid.F(:)) = true; Eid = E(isTripleLine & all(onGrain(E),2),:); X = [V(Eid(:,1),1), V(Eid(:,2),1), nan(size(Eid,1),1)].'; Y = [V(Eid(:,1),2), V(Eid(:,2),2), nan(size(Eid,1),1)].'; Z = [V(Eid(:,1),3), V(Eid(:,2),3), nan(size(Eid,1),1)].'; plot(gBid,'FaceColor',[0.85 0.85 0.85],'LineStyle','none','micronbar','off') hold on line(X(:),Y(:),Z(:),'Color','r','LineWidth',1.5) isQuad = mod(t,10) >= 4 & onGrain; scatter3(V(isQuad,1),V(isQuad,2),V(isQuad,3),30,'b','filled') hold off setCamera(how2plot) {% endhighlight %}

The node types say how much of the network is junction. Most vertices lie inside a face, one in eight on a triple line, and about one in a hundred at a quadruple point; the classes above 10 are the same on the hull.

{% highlight matlab %} n = accumarray(t(t>0),1); [find(n), n(n>0)] {% endhighlight %} {% highlight plaintext %} ans = 2 241422 3 41328 4 3806 5 167 6 4 11 46985 12 11912 13 1078 14 27 {% endhighlight %}

From faces to the boundary character

Each face carries all five parameters of a grain boundary: the misorientation of the two grains and the normal of the face. The normals of the voxel surface point along the axes, so the surface has to be smoothed first, see Smoothing, before the boundary normal distribution or the boundary character distribution reads anything but the voxel grid.

{% highlight matlab %} grainsS = smoothBoundary(reduceBoundary(grains,2,'quadric'),taubinFilter(20)); gBS = grainsS.boundary('indexed'); plot(calcGBND(gBS),'upper','micronbar','off') mtexColorbar {% endhighlight %}
